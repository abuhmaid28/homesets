1. Product vision and exact problem statement
Vision: a private, offline-first Android app that builds a short, realistic muscle-building plan around the user's real equipment and time, runs the workout with minimal friction, and shows real progress (photos + numbers) so the user keeps going.
Problem (Ahmed's words, distilled): he wants to build muscle and a better-looking physique at home because he cannot rely on a gym. In the past he had no schedule, got bored, and did not see progress, so he stopped. The app must therefore solve: (1) what to do today without thinking, (2) progressing correctly without guessing, (3) seeing progress even when the scale moves slowly, (4) surviving irregular days without guilt.
Not the goal: another generic workout app with many features. Optimize for daily usefulness, not feature count.

4. Evidence-informed training and progression requirements
Treat these as guidelines to be re-verified by you, not guarantees. Distinguish science-backed principles from product choices.
Principles [Recommended]:
• Muscle growth is driven mainly by progressive overload with sets taken close to failure and enough weekly volume, supported by adequate calories/protein and sleep.
• Train each major muscle about 2 times per week and aim for roughly 10+ hard sets per muscle per week as a starting target, adjusted by response and time.
• Work most sets at about 1–3 reps in reserve (RIR). Rest about 1–3 minutes between sets (shorter rests for isolation work if time is tight).
• With only 15–30 minutes, prefer 4 short sessions per week over 3 long ones. The engine decides the final count from time, level and goal.
• Bands are not weight plates: progression comes from more reps in a range, slower tempo, higher band tension, harder variations (e.g., feet-elevated, archer or one-arm push-up progressions, weighted-style pull-up variations are not available), and unilateral work. Legs are the weak point with bands only; use single-leg and tempo variations and flag the limit honestly.
Starting exercise pool (to be reviewed and tagged by you): pull-ups, chin-ups, slow negatives, hanging knee raises; push-up variations (incline, decline, tempo, band-resisted, archer progressions); band row, band face pull, band pull-apart, band pulldown anchored to the bar; band chest press, band overhead press, band lateral raise, band curl, band triceps pushdown/overhead extension; band squat, split squat/Bulgarian split squat (needs a stable chair), band Romanian deadlift, glute bridge/hip thrust with band, calf raise; plank variations.
Nutrition and weight (simple, no calorie counting) [Recommended]: a daily 4–5 meal checklist and an easy list of cheap protein sources (eggs, liver, foul, lentils, milk, yogurt, tuna, chicken, peanut butter, rice, oats, bananas, dates). Weekly weight check-in under similar conditions, shown as a weekly average trend. Rules in Section 11.

11. Progression logic, edge cases, and safety guardrails
Progression (double progression) [Recommended]: each exercise has a rep range (e.g., 8–15) and a difficulty ladder (variation/tempo/band tension). If all sets reach the top of the range with feedback Easy/Right, advance one rung next time; if Hard with falling reps, hold or step back; if Easy on every set, advance faster. Never advance two things at once.
Recovery spacing: do not schedule sessions that hit the same primary muscles within about 24–48 hours; allow override with a clear warning.
Missed days: no scolding, no streak loss. Resume at the next session. After about 7+ days away, start with a lighter re-entry session. After a long gap (several weeks), reduce volume/intensity for the first sessions.
Lite mode: a 15-minute version of the next session (fewer exercises, key movements first). Lite counts toward weekly consistency.
Plateaus: if an exercise stalls for several sessions, change the rung, tempo or variation before adding volume; surface the reason in plain Arabic.
Weight trend rules: weekly average; if gaining goal and average change is under about 0.1 kg/week for 3 weeks, nudge to add a meal/portion; if weight drops while the goal is gaining, nudge gently. If weight does not rise despite sustained extra eating for several weeks, or loss is rapid and unexplained, suggest a normal medical check-up. No BMI labels, no shaming language, no diagnosis.
Safety guardrails: areas marked as painful remove matching exercises; pain during a set offers stop/swap; clear one-time notice that the app is not medical advice; conservative defaults for unknown fitness level; no extreme programs, no unsafe loading.
Edge cases to specify and test: first session ever; changing equipment mid-plan; swapping an exercise permanently vs once; deleting a session; clock/timezone changes; phone killed mid-workout (resume safely); import of an older backup; very low or very high max-rep inputs.
